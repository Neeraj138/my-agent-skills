# Sheets, critique, and motion review

## Produce evidence the reviewer can inspect

Render images from the actual scene code and actual narration timeline when available. Label sheet thumbnails with beat, time, and state in the contact-sheet margin; those labels are review metadata, not video overlays.

For a substantial narrated film, include initial, middle, and settled states for every beat. For difficult mechanisms, add per-sentence frames and intermediate multiply, travel, merge, and commit states. Sample important boundaries at about 25/50/75% of the transition and add arrival/end samples when needed. Save native-size close-ups of dense states and inspect at the expected viewing size too.

Use a small representative pilot to establish the visual language before propagating it. An arithmetic pilot does not establish how human illustrations, hardware, or queues should look. Review those motifs in their own representative scenes.

Keep review rounds in separate folders. Record which image or source revision a verdict covers; hashes are useful for larger pipelines. Preserve prior verdicts as historical evidence, not current approval. A script pass, old scene pass, or successful decode cannot approve a revised image.

## Fresh critic brief

Use a fresh-context reviewer when requested or when the work is substantial enough to benefit. Follow the user's model choice and delegation limits. Give the reviewer the brief, audience, narration, current images, and relevant source facts. Ask it to view the actual images rather than reviewing filenames or source code alone.

A suitable prompt:

> Review these Excalidraw-style explainer sheets as a first-time viewer and visual director. The audience and output constraints are attached. Inspect the images at native and intended viewing size. Check explanatory hierarchy, illustration craft, arithmetic, object identity, visual ownership, label clearance, and the stated transitions. For each failure, name the beat/time, what the viewer would misunderstand or struggle to read, the smallest adequate correction or required recomposition, and how to verify it. Separate release blockers from useful refinements. Give an explicit scoped verdict and list the files inspected. Do not infer motion quality from stills.

A useful critique is finite and actionable. “Make it polished” is not an implementation direction. Ask for spatial anchors or choreography when a mechanism needs recomposition. Apply valid findings and explain any evidence-based disagreement. Do not endlessly add ornament or universal restrictions to satisfy a vague preference.

## Review checklist

- Can the viewer identify the current operation without reading a paragraph?
- Does each spoken claim have a matching action or a deliberate explanatory hold?
- Are operands and results readable, numerically correct, and clearly associated?
- Do guides, payloads, labels, and figures stay clear throughout motion?
- Does the same object keep its values, owner, shape convention, and meaning?
- Are stale diagrams retired, with no competing complete-diagram double exposure?
- Does a close-up simplify the concept rather than merely magnify clutter?
- Does the illustration have continuous contours, correct occlusion, and purposeful detail?
- Does the film alternate useful context and close-up distance rather than repeat one layout?
- Are forbidden or unrequested decorative overlays absent?

## Smooth handoffs

A crossfade is not automatically a clear transition. Two complete diagrams at half opacity can be less legible than either original.

When an object is genuinely shared, retain it, retire the outgoing machinery, move or reframe it, then reveal the next operation. Exclude duplicate copies from both scene groups. Allow the retained object to become large enough to carry attention while isolated.

When numerical objects differ, do not morph them as if their values were unchanged. Carry a correctly identified token or other context anchor, clear the old example, and introduce the new example explicitly. Choose a clean staged reveal when no shared object exists. Respect requests for smooth transitions without forcing unrelated diagrams into a false continuous calculation.

## Motion gate

After sheet corrections, render short native-timing proofs of the hardest operations and boundaries. Watch them with narration: stills miss speed, unreadable transit, premature commits, abrupt switches, and clipping between samples.

Check the first worked example at teaching speed, then repetitions. Verify that the destination changes after arrival, source rows remain interpretable, and the final state settles long enough to read. Fix failures before full exports. For a user-requested sheet-first series, approve every required episode sheet before starting complete-film production; representative pilot approval alone is insufficient.

If the reviewer is unavailable, report the actual gap. Continue useful independent work, but do not claim its review occurred or bypass an explicit review-before-render requirement.
