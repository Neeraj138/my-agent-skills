# English: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

All English that the agent writes for the piece follows ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English): voiceover scripts, captions, labels, cover titles, slide text and speaker notes. STE makes text clear for every viewer, including viewers who do not speak English as a first language, and it makes TTS voices and subtitles more accurate.

If the user asks for a different style or language, follow the user. Facts and technical names do not change; only the sentence form changes.

## Words
- Use simple, common words, with one meaning for each word. Use "start", not "initiate"; "use", not "utilize"; "show", not "demonstrate"; "about", not "approximately"; "help", not "facilitate".
- Use the same word for the same thing every time. If you say "white blood cell" in beat 2, do not say "leukocyte" in beat 5 unless you introduce it ("white blood cells, or leukocytes").
- **Technical names** (neutrophil, selectin, reciprocal lattice, GPU) and **technical verbs** of the field (phagocytose, diffract, compile) are permitted. They are part of the depth that the user chose in the brief.
- Do not use idioms, slang, or phrasal verbs that have a simple equivalent ("set up" → "make"; "figure out" → "find"; "kick in" → "start").
- Do not make a noun cluster of more than three words ("post-capillary venule wall junction protein" → "a protein in the wall of the small vein").
- Do not use contractions in the script or on screen ("it is", "do not", not "it's", "don't").

## Verbs
- Use the active voice: "The neutrophil moves through the wall," not "The wall is crossed by the neutrophil."
- Use simple tenses: present, past and future ("moves", "moved", "will move"). Do not use "has been moving" or "would have moved".
- Do not use "-ing" forms as verbs or nouns ("Swelling occurs" → "The tissue swells"). An "-ing" word that is a technical name is permitted ("rolling" as a named step of leukocyte recruitment).
- Use a verb for an action, not a noun made from a verb: "The cells stick to the wall," not "Adhesion of the cells to the wall takes place."

## Sentences
- One topic in each sentence.
- Maximum 20 words in a sentence that tells the viewer what to do, and maximum 25 words in a descriptive sentence. For narration, try for 8–16 words; the calm pace needs short sentences.
- Use articles and demonstratives ("the", "a", "this"). Do not delete words to make text shorter, except in short on-screen labels.
- Put the most important information first.
- A paragraph (a beat) has one topic and a maximum of six sentences.
- Write numbers as words in the voice script (for TTS), and as numerals on screen.

## Questions and hooks
STE permits questions. A hook question is good STE if it is short and simple: "Why does a wound swell?" A rhetorical question with no answer in the video is not.

## Check every script before the voice step
- [ ] Each sentence has 25 words or fewer (most have 16 or fewer).
- [ ] Active voice, simple tenses, no "-ing" verbs except technical names.
- [ ] No contractions, idioms or phrasal verbs.
- [ ] One word for each thing, from start to end.
- [ ] Each technical name is introduced before it is used, at the depth from the brief.

Quick length check (prints sentences longer than 20 words):
```bash
sed 's/<break[^>]*>//g' out/voice/<id>.txt | tr '\n' ' ' | grep -oE '[^.?!]+[.?!]' | awk 'NF>20 {print NF": "$0}'
```
